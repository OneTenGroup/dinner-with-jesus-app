### Dinner 1 — Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 — "We Weren't Made to Do This Alone"
*Upgrade of existing active dinner (id `1966552e-827c-4af1-b046-5123b7e410bb`). Category: Friendship/Community.*

**Scripture:** Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their labor. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow; but woe to him who is alone when he falls, and doesn't have another to lift him up.

**A Little Context:** This is part of a longer reflection on what actually makes life worth living. Right in the middle of it, the writer stops to make a simple, practical point: doing life with someone is better than doing it alone. It isn't a poem about romance — it's about anyone who actually has your back.

**For the Table Tonight:** Who's the one person who'd genuinely show up if you called them at 2am?

**Go Deeper:** Is there someone in your life right now who's trying to do something hard alone — who you know needs to be pulled in — and you haven't done anything about it yet?

**Push Further:** The passage doesn't say two are better than one because life is dangerous — it says so because "if they fall." Falling is treated as a given, not a maybe. What does it change to plan for the fall in advance, instead of assuming you'll be the exception who doesn't need anyone?

**Prayer:** God, thank you for the people who've lifted us up when we've fallen — we don't say that out loud enough. Show us tonight who around us is trying to stand alone, and give us the nerve to reach out first. Amen.

### Dinner 2 — Mark 9:21-24 — "Bring Jesus Your Doubt Too"
*Upgrade of existing active dinner (id `a47aa3ae-2636-4da5-a123-3d1c9acc5eef`), expanded from v.24 alone to include the father's full exchange with Jesus. Category: Faith/Doubt.*

**Scripture:** He asked his father, "How long has it been since this has come to him?" He said, "From childhood. Often it has cast him both into the fire and into the water, to destroy him. But if you can do anything, have compassion on us, and help us." Jesus said to him, "If you can believe, all things are possible to him who believes." Immediately the father of the child cried out with tears, "I believe. Help my unbelief!"

**A Little Context:** This father has watched his son suffer through violent seizures his entire childhood. He's exhausted, and by the time he reaches Jesus, so is his faith. What he says next is one of the most honest sentences in the Gospels.

**For the Table Tonight:** Have you ever wanted to believe something was going to work out, but part of you just didn't? What did that feel like?

**Go Deeper:** Where in your life right now are you saying "I believe" out loud while a quieter voice inside you is saying "help me actually believe it"?

**Push Further:** Watch where the word "if" moves in this exchange — the father starts with "if you can do anything," and by his own last sentence it's become "help my unbelief," as if the uncertainty has shifted from Jesus's ability to his own. Whether or not that's exactly what's happening in the father's mind, Jesus heals the boy anyway, doubt and all — what does that tell you about what Jesus actually requires before he acts?

**Prayer:** Jesus, we believe — and we need help with the part of us that doesn't. We're not going to pretend to be more certain than we are tonight. Meet us exactly where our faith actually is, not where we wish it was. Amen.

### Dinner 3 — Psalm 34:17-18 — "God Comes Close"
*Upgrade of existing active dinner (id `d3c660fb-2608-42d1-8e83-fc60eae5053a`), expanded to include v.17. Category: Hope/Grief.*

**Scripture:** The righteous cry, and Yahweh hears, And delivers them out of all their troubles. Yahweh is near to those who have a broken heart, And saves those who have a crushed spirit.

**A Little Context:** David wrote this while running for his life, having just faked insanity to escape a king who wanted him dead. This isn't written from safety looking back — it's written from inside the wreckage.

**For the Table Tonight:** When you're going through something hard, do you tend to pull people in close, or push everyone away?

**Go Deeper:** When someone you love is hurting, do you tend to move toward them, try to fix them, or become uncomfortable and pull away? Why?

**Push Further:** This verse doesn't say God is near to the strong or the composed — it says he's near specifically to the broken. What does it reveal about who God actually is, that brokenness draws him close instead of pushing him away?

**Prayer:** God, you say you're near to the brokenhearted, so let's not perform having it together tonight if we don't. Whatever's crushed in this room right now, come close to it, exactly like you promised. Amen.

### Dinner 4 — Matthew 5:43-45 — "Love the Person Who Doesn't Love You"
*New. Category: Love (enemies).*

**Scripture:** "You have heard that it was said, 'You shall love your neighbor, and hate your enemy.' But I tell you, love your enemies, bless those who curse you, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who mistreat you and persecute you, that you may be children of your Father who is in heaven. For he makes his sun to rise on the evil and the good, and sends rain on the just and the unjust.

**A Little Context:** Jesus is correcting a common assumption of his day — that loving your neighbor also gave you permission to hate your enemy. He doesn't just forbid the hate. He commands the exact opposite of it.

**For the Table Tonight:** Who's someone — doesn't have to be dramatic — that's genuinely hard for you to be kind to right now?

**Go Deeper:** What's actually stopping you from praying for that specific person, by name, sometime this week?

**Push Further:** Jesus grounds this command in something surprising — not "because it's the right thing to do," but because it's literally how God acts, sending sun and rain on everyone regardless of what they deserve. If loving your enemy is what makes you look like your Father, what does it say about a faith that only extends love to people who've earned it?

**Prayer:** God, you send sun and rain on people who never say thank you, and you're asking us to love like that too. We name the person we're struggling with tonight, even just in our own heads, and ask you to help us want good for them, not just tolerate them. Amen.

### Dinner 5 — James 1:19-20 — "Listen Before You Win"
*New. Category: Anger.*

**Scripture:** So, then, my beloved brothers, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger; for the anger of man doesn't produce the righteousness of God.

**A Little Context:** James is writing to a scattered, struggling church, and before he tackles almost anything else, he starts here — with how they actually talk to each other.

**For the Table Tonight:** Are you usually the person in an argument who's already forming your response while the other person is still talking?

**Go Deeper:** Think of the last time you got genuinely angry — did it get you closer to what you actually wanted, or further away?

**Push Further:** James puts these in a specific order — quick to listen, slow to speak, slow to anger — not the order most arguments actually run in. What do you think changes in a conversation the moment your goal shifts from winning it to actually understanding the other person?

**Prayer:** God, we're quicker to speak than to listen more often than we'd like to admit. Slow us down tonight, in this conversation and in the ones we're avoiding, and show us where our anger's been doing more talking than you ever asked it to. Amen.

### Dinner 6 — Matthew 6:19-21 — "Your Heart Follows Your Treasure"
*New. Category: Money.*

**Scripture:** "Don't lay up treasures for yourselves on the earth, where moth and rust consume, and where thieves break through and steal; but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust consume, and where thieves don't break through and steal; for where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.

**A Little Context:** Jesus isn't saying money itself is evil. He's making an observation about direction — whatever you actually invest in ends up owning your attention, whether you meant it to or not.

**For the Table Tonight:** If someone looked at your bank statement and your calendar from the last month, what would they guess you care about most?

**Go Deeper:** Is there something you're pouring real time or money into that, if you're honest, isn't going to matter in twenty years?

**Push Further:** Jesus says the heart follows the treasure — not the other way around. Most of us assume we should feel more spiritual first, and our spending would follow. What would it look like to actually reverse that — to move the treasure first and trust your heart to catch up?

**Prayer:** God, show us tonight where our treasure's actually been going, even the parts we haven't wanted to look at closely. We want our hearts to follow you — help us move what we treasure first, and trust you with the rest. Amen.

**Push Further:** Jesus doesn't say "blessed are you for knowing this." He says blessed are you if you actually do it. Why do you think he draws such a hard line between understanding this and actually living it out?

### Dinner 10 — John 8:3-11 — "Grace Without Stones"
*New. Category: Grace/Judgment.*

**Scripture:** The scribes and the Pharisees brought a woman taken in adultery. Having set her in the midst, they told him, "Teacher, we found this woman in adultery, in the very act. Now in our law, Moses commanded us to stone such. What then do you say about her?" They said this testing him, that they might have something to accuse him of. But Jesus stooped down, and wrote on the ground with his finger. But when they continued asking him, he looked up and said to them, "He who is without sin among you, let him throw the first stone at her." Jesus said, "Neither do I condemn you. Go your way. From now on, sin no more."

**A Little Context:** This woman is a trap — a setup to catch Jesus, not an act of real justice. She's been dragged into public with genuine guilt, and real people are standing there ready to legally kill her, right now.

**For the Table Tonight:** Has someone ever caught you at your absolute worst moment? What did they do with that?

**Go Deeper:** Is there someone you've quietly written off because of something they did, and you've never really reconsidered it since?

**Push Further:** Jesus never says what she did was okay — he still tells her to "sin no more." But he also refuses to pick up a stone. What's the difference between the truth Jesus tells her and the judgment the crowd was ready to deliver — and which one actually changes a person?

**Prayer:** Jesus, thank you that you tell us the truth without ever picking up a stone. We're carrying rocks tonight for people we've already decided aren't worth a second look — help us put them down, and give us the same kind of honesty that leads somewhere good instead of just condemning. Amen.

### Dinner 11 — Genesis 2:18-24 — "It Is Not Good to Be Alone"
*New. Category: Marriage.*

**Scripture:** Yahweh God said, "It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him a helper suitable for him." Out of the ground Yahweh God formed every animal of the field, and every bird of the sky, and brought them to the man to see what he would call them. Whatever the man called every living creature, that was its name. The man gave names to all cattle, and to the birds of the sky, and to every animal of the field; but for man there was not found a helper suitable for him. Yahweh God caused a deep sleep to fall on the man, and he slept; and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh in its place. He made the rib, which Yahweh God had taken from the man, into a woman, and brought her to the man. The man said, "This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh. **A Little Context:** This happens before anything has gone wrong in the world — before sin, before shame, before any of it. In an otherwise "very good" creation, God himself is the one who says something isn't good yet: the man is alone. The woman is made as a partner who corresponds to him, and the passage ends with the two joined as one.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something small your spouse — or, if you're not married, someone in a marriage you admire — does that makes you feel truly known?

**Go Deeper:** Where in your closest relationship have you been more focused on being understood than on actually understanding the other person?

**Push Further:** Before God makes the woman, he has Adam name every animal first — and none of them turn out to be "suitable for him." Why do you think God let Adam feel that lack before giving him the answer, instead of just skipping ahead to it?

**Prayer:** God, being fully known by another person instead of left alone is a real gift, and we don't want to take it for granted. Where our closest relationships have grown distant or distracted, draw us back toward "bone of my bones" — toward actually being one, not just living side by side. Amen.

**A Little Context:** This psalm is about building — houses, cities, families — and right in the middle of it, the psalm calls children not a cost but a reward, arrows a parent aims deliberately toward the future.

**Prayer:** God, thank you for the children at this table — the little ones, and the ones who used to be. On the days it feels like more weight than gift, remind us what you actually called this. Help us aim well, not just survive the week. Amen.

### Dinner 13 — John 8:34-36 — "Free Indeed"
*New. Category: Addiction & Recovery.*

**Scripture:** Jesus answered them, "Most assuredly I tell you, everyone who commits sin is the bondservant of sin. A bondservant doesn't live in the house forever. A son remains forever. If therefore the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed.

**A Little Context:** Jesus is talking to people proud of their freedom — they insist they've never technically been slaves to anyone. He isn't talking about that kind of slavery. He's naming something everyone in the room, and everyone at every table since, actually recognizes.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something — it doesn't have to be dramatic — that you know isn't good for you, but you keep going back to anyway?

**Go Deeper:** Is there a pattern in your life right now — big or small, no need to name it out loud — that you've tried to muscle through alone instead of actually letting anyone help you with?

**Push Further:** Jesus draws a sharp line — a slave doesn't stay in the family forever, but a son does, permanently. Being set free doesn't mean the pull disappears overnight, and people walking in real freedom often still do hard, ongoing work to stay there — support, honesty, accountability. What's the difference between white-knuckling a habit alone and being free in a way that's still allowed to include a real fight?

**Prayer:** Jesus, we bring you what has a grip on us that shouldn't — even the parts we're not ready to say out loud tonight. You call that freedom even while the fight for it is still real. Thank you that we don't have to win it alone first before it counts as grace. Amen.

### Dinner 19 — Mark 10:13-16 — "Let the Children Come"

**Scripture:** They were bringing to him little children, that he should touch them, but the disciples rebuked those who were bringing them. But when Jesus saw it, he was moved with indignation, and said to them, "Allow the little children to come to me! Don't forbid them, for the Kingdom of God belongs to such as these. Most assuredly I tell you, whoever will not receive the Kingdom of God like a little child, he will in no way enter into it." He took them in his arms, and blessed them, laying his hands on them.

**A Little Context:** The disciples think they're protecting Jesus's time by turning families away. Jesus becomes genuinely indignant about it, and does the exact opposite of what his own followers wanted.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something a child has said or done recently — yours, a niece or nephew, anyone — that stuck with you?

**Go Deeper:** When was the last time you approached God the way a little kid approaches someone they trust completely, without pretending, without an agenda?

**Push Further:** Jesus gets genuinely indignant that his own disciples are the ones keeping children away from him. What does it say about how easily even well-meaning faith can get in the way of the exact people Jesus wants closest to him?

**Prayer:** Jesus, you got angry on behalf of the children the world tried to push aside, and you still do. Help this family raise kids who feel like the Kingdom actually belongs to them, and help the adults at this table remember what it felt like to trust you that simply. Amen.

### Dinner 20 — Luke 5:27-32 — "Dinner With Sinners"
*New. Category: Jesus & Outsiders.*

**Scripture:** After these things he went out, and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax office, and said to him, "Follow me!" He left everything, and rose up and followed him. Levi made a great feast for him in his house. There was a great crowd of tax collectors and others who were reclining with them. Their scribes and the Pharisees murmured against his disciples, saying, "Why do you eat and drink with the tax collectors and sinners?" Jesus answered them, "Those who are healthy have no need for a physician, but those who are sick do. I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance."

**A Little Context:** Tax collectors in Jesus's day were considered traitors — Jewish men collecting money for Rome, often skimming extra for themselves. Levi is exactly the kind of person religious people avoided, and Jesus doesn't just tolerate him — he goes to his house and eats at his table.

**For the Table Tonight:** Is there a type of person — not one specific individual, just a "type" — you'd be a little uncomfortable seeing show up at this table?

**Go Deeper:** Have you ever been the outsider in a room, the one everyone assumed had it together less than they did? What was that like?

**Push Further:** The religious leaders' complaint isn't really about Levi — it's about who Jesus is willing to be seen with. If the company Jesus kept tells you something about his actual character, what does it say, and does the company you keep say the same thing about yours?

**Prayer:** Jesus, you went looking for the person everyone else had already written off, and you sat down at his table instead of waiting for him to clean up first. There's someone like that in our own lives this week — bring their face to mind, and give us the nerve to actually pull up a chair. Amen.

## Revision Report — Editorial Pass 2

*The scoring table from the first draft has been removed per instruction. What follows is editorial judgment, not a numeric self-grade.*

### Which dinners changed, and why

**#1 — Ecclesiastes 4:9-10.** Removed "the richest, wisest man who ever lived" and the invented biographical claim that this was written after Solomon personally tried to build a life on his own achievement — neither is something the text itself asserts, and both read as dramatic embellishment. Context now stays inside what the passage actually says. Push Further rewritten to stop leaning on the removed superlative and instead notice something the text really does say: it assumes falling, not just recommends friendship as a nice-to-have.

**#2 — Mark 9:21-24.** Core preserved (this was and remains one of the strongest dinners in the batch). The reading that the "if" shifts from Jesus's ability to the father's belief is now presented as an observation to trace in the dialogue ("watch where the word 'if' moves"), with an explicit "whether or not that's exactly what's happening" hedge, instead of being asserted as the passage's plain meaning.

**#3 — Psalm 34:17-18.** Go Deeper replaced with your suggested alternative — it was pressuring people toward disclosing a specific private pain rather than opening a real conversation. New version asks how people generally respond to someone else's pain, which anyone can answer regardless of what they're currently carrying. Prayer's "we're not going to pretend" line softened for variety (see global pass below).

**#4 — Matthew 5:43-45.** Unchanged. Reviewed for accuracy; nothing to fix.

**#5 — James 1:19-20.** Push Further rewritten to actually engage the specific order James gives (listen → speak → anger) rather than a more generic point about anger's purpose. This is a genuine strengthening, not just a rephrase.

**#6 — Matthew 6:19-21.** Unchanged. Reviewed for accuracy; the "heart follows treasure" claim is the verse's own stated logic, not an overreach.

**#7 — John 13:12-17.** One-word hedge added ("considered a job for the lowest servant") on the foot-washing custom, since it's background information, not something the text states outright — low-risk to begin with, but now consistent with the new rule.

**#8 — Luke 15:17-24.** Removed the claim about a father running in public being culturally undignified and needing to hike up his robe. This is a real, commonly-taught point in Middle Eastern cultural-background scholarship, but it isn't in the text, and we don't have it sourced to production standard — so it's gone, not softened. Replaced with the actual textual detail, which does the same work: he saw him "while he was still far off" and was already running before the son said a word. Honestly, the dinner is not weaker for this change — the plain text was always doing the real work.

**#9 — Philippians 2:3-5.** This needed the most repair. The claim that Paul wrote this "in the middle of asking two leaders to get along" was wrong as placed — Euodia and Syntyche are named in chapter 4, not adjacent to this passage — and has been removed rather than relocated. Context now states only what's true: Paul opens a letter to a church he loves with a plea about how they treat each other. Opener rewritten from a generic "who do you admire" question to one that actually tracks the passage's specific content (noticing what others need instead of what you need).

**#10 — John 8:3-11.** Unchanged. Reviewed for accuracy; the "trap, not justice" framing and the stoning-law reference are both directly supported by the text.

**#11 — Genesis 2:18-24.** Removed "marriage is God's answer to loneliness" — too broad, and it risks implying single people are incomplete or that marriage cures loneliness, neither of which the passage claims. Context now stays with what the text actually does: names the man's aloneness as the one "not good" in a very-good creation, and follows the narrative to the woman as a corresponding partner and the two becoming one. The questions were left as marriage-focused (that's the intended topic) but the universal claim is gone.

**#12 — Psalm 127:3-5.** "Solomon calls children..." changed to "the psalm calls children..." — psalm superscriptions attributing authorship are a real scholarly dispute, not settled fact, and the point doesn't need the author's name to land. Push Further sharpened slightly (an arrow's direction is fixed before release, not after) without inventing complexity the short passage can't support.

**#13 — John 8:34-36.** The dinner most in need of care, and the one where the instruction to "tell us if something still isn't great" matters most: this is now materially different, not just word-smoothed. Removed "not partway, not managed, free" from the prayer — that phrasing could read as promising instant, effortless transformation, which is not a responsible thing to put in front of a family with someone in active recovery. Push Further now explicitly says freedom doesn't mean the pull vanishes overnight, and that people in real recovery still do ongoing, real work — support, honesty, accountability — without that work meaning they aren't "really" free. Go Deeper no longer asks people to measure how ashamed they are of something they "wouldn't want to say out loud" — it asks about a pattern they've tried to handle alone instead of asking for help, which lets someone answer honestly about something small (screen time, snapping at their kids) or something serious, without the question itself forcing the size of the disclosure.

**#14 — Luke 12:16-21.** Unchanged. Reviewed for accuracy; nothing to fix.

**#15 — 1 Corinthians 15:3-7, 20-22.** Removed two claims that were doing apologetics rather than description: that the five hundred witnesses were "available to be asked directly," and that this is "one of the oldest passages in the New Testament, written within a couple of decades." Both are defensible scholarly positions, but they're historical-critical arguments, not something this context box should be asserting as settled background. Context now simply states what Paul says he's handing on: Christ died, was buried, was raised, appeared to specific people. Opener adjusted so it isn't built entirely around uncertainty about death — it now asks about a memory first, which still opens real grief conversation without implying the passage (or the prayer that follows it) has nothing to say in response.

**#16 — Matthew 7:1-5.** Unchanged in substance. One phrase in Push Further was reworded during the global pass to reduce a repeated construction — see below.

**#17 — Matthew 6:31-33.** Prayer rewritten. The original ("we spend so much of each day chasing what we'll eat and wear before we ever get around to seeking you") reads as mild guilt-tripping in exactly the way the note warned about. New version leads with invitation, not accusation.

**#18 — Proverbs 15:1.** Push Further strengthened once, as instructed, without manufacturing depth a one-line proverb can't hold: it now asks what a gentle answer actually does (removes the fuel anger needs) rather than just contrasting it with avoidance.

**#19 — Mark 10:13-16.** Removed "one of the few moments the Gospels describe him that specifically" — an unverified frequency claim that added nothing. The text's own fact (Jesus became indignant) carries the point without it.

**#20 — Luke 5:27-32.** Unchanged, including the Push Further you specifically asked us to preserve. Reviewed for accuracy; the tax-collector background is standard, uncontested historical context.

### Global pass — patterns found and fixed across all 20 together

- **Prayer openers.** After the individual edits above, 6 of 20 prayers still opened with some form of "thank you" (#1, #8, #10, #12, #19, plus the pattern was already present in #11). Revised #11 and #19 to remove the construction while keeping the sentiment, bringing it down to 4 of 20 — a reasonable spread, not a tic.
- **"What's the difference between X and Y" Push Further construction.** Found in 4 of 20 (#9, #10, #13, #16). Revised #16 to a different shape ("does the same correction feel different to give — and different to receive?"). Left the other three as-is: three uses across twenty genuinely different dinners is a real but minor pattern, not yet a rut — worth watching if it keeps showing up at this rate in Batch 02.
- **"Not X — Y" contrastive construction** (the specific tic flagged in the original content audit's read of the existing `prayer_level_2/3` material): not present anywhere in this batch. This was a real risk given that material was offered as source content, and it didn't leak in.
- **Adult-coded phrasing that a teenager can technically still answer but wasn't written with them first in mind:** #6's "bank statement and calendar" and #14's "one more truly great financial year" both lean adult. Neither is unanswerable by a teenager (both include an accessible half — the calendar in #6, the imaginative "if you suddenly had money" framing in #14), but we're flagging this honestly rather than calling it fixed. Worth a specific pass in Batch 02 on money-themed dinners specifically, since money is one of the harder topics to phrase for a 14-year-old and a 45-year-old at the same table.

1. **#2** — the "if" reading. Now phrased as an observation with a hedge, but it's still an interpretive lens on the dialogue, not a neutral fact. Worth a pastor's read.
2. **#3** — the David/Achish backstory (faking madness to escape a king who wanted him dead). This is directly narrated in 1 Samuel 21 and matches Psalm 34's own superscription, so risk is low, but it should still get the same citation check as everything else before going live.
