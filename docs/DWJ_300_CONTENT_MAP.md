# Dinner With Jesus 2.0 — 300-Dinner Content Map

**Status:** Planning document only. Nothing in this file has been written to `public.dinner_verses`. No content is active, inactive, created, edited, or deleted as a result of this document.

**Purpose:** Map the existing 119 active dinners, then design ~181 new dinner slots to reach ~300, so the full library can be reviewed for balance before any content is drafted at full quality. Per the editorial brief, **new slots deliberately do not add more Hope/Faith/Wisdom** (already 40% of the current library) — they target genuine gaps.

**Translation note (checked before any Scripture drafting began):** all existing `dinner_verses.verse_text` is in the **World English Bible (WEB)** — confirmed by textual fingerprint ("Yahweh," modern contractions) and cross-checked against this app's own `bible_verses` table and prior internal audit (`docs/DWJ_PRODUCT_CONTENT_CHURCH_STRATEGY_2026-07-16.md`, §5: *"KJV and WEB are both public domain... NIV/NLT/ESV/NKJV are commercially licensed translations. Do not add any of them without a licensing review."*). Independently verified via web search: the WEB is dedicated to the public domain by its translators (translation work 1994–2020), with no copyright restriction on reproduction — confirmed against ebible.org's own copyright page. **No licensing issue exists for the 300-dinner expansion as long as it stays in WEB** (or KJV, also public domain). This map and Batch 01 both use WEB throughout, pulled directly from this app's own `bible_verses` table for accuracy rather than composed from memory. **Do not silently switch translations** — if NIV/NLT/ESV/NKJV content is ever wanted, that requires a separate licensing review first, not a content decision.

### Bible-book balance

- **53 of 66 canonical books represented** across the full 300 (up from 34). The 13 with zero representation: Leviticus, Judges, 1 Chronicles, Ezra, Ezekiel, Amos, Jonah, Nahum, Haggai, Zechariah, Titus, 2 John, Jude. Deliberately not forced in — per your own instruction not to force categories Scripture doesn't support. Worth flagging for a *future* batch, not this one: Jonah (mercy toward an enemy nation, obedience) and Ezekiel 37 (dry bones, hope/renewal) are both strong, accessible candidates if broader book coverage becomes a priority later.
- **Old Testament: 122 dinners / New Testament: 178 dinners** (41% / 59%). This is a deliberate NT lean, consistent with "Keep Jesus Central" — but the OT total is still substantial and spans law, history, wisdom, and all the major and several minor prophets.
- **Gospels specifically (Matthew/Mark/Luke/John): 76 of 300 (25%)** — up from the current library's Gospel share, and includes a dedicated 10-dinner cluster of material that is unambiguously about who Jesus is and what he did, not just his recorded teachings repurposed for a different topic.

### Direct Jesus-centered content

Beyond the 76 Gospel-book dinners generally, dinners that put Jesus's own words, actions, or character at the direct center (not just cited in passing) include: eating with sinners, the woman caught in adultery, Zacchaeus, the woman at the well, calming the storm, Gethsemane, "Father, forgive them," the resurrection appearances, foot-washing, the call of Levi, the temptation in the wilderness, "I am the resurrection and the life," the thief on the cross, and more — roughly **35-40 dinners** where Jesus himself, not a topic he's cited to support, is the actual subject of the conversation.

- **Zero repeated Scripture references within the new 181** (validated programmatically).

### Emotional range

The new 181 include dinners designed to: make people laugh (Proverbs 6:6-8's ant, several humor-note-adjacent one-liners once drafted), debate (Acts 15's Paul/Barnabas split, 1 Corinthians 6:12's "lawful but not helpful"), feel grateful (the one leper who returned), sit with real discomfort (Matthew 23's "clean on the outside," the pride cluster), consider an apology (the whole Reconciliation cluster, Matthew 5:23-24), expose pride (Luke 18's Pharisee and tax collector, Daniel 4), offer hope without forcing resolution (Psalm 88, deliberately left unresolved), admit doubt (Thomas, Habakkuk, John the Baptist from prison), help someone forgive (Luke 23:34, Philemon), make parents listen (the entire Parenting cluster), make teenagers want to participate (1 Timothy 4:12, "let no one despise your youth," addressed directly to them), help families understand each other (Marriage, Conflict, Reconciliation), reveal something new about Jesus (the Jesus-centered cluster generally), and go genuinely deeply emotional (Psalm 88, 2 Samuel 18:33, John 11's "Jesus wept," Gethsemane).

### Family relevance & age accessibility

Every new entry was chosen with a real, ordinary dinner-table moment in mind, not an abstract doctrine to cover — this was the actual selection filter, not a retrofit. A genuine risk to flag honestly: **Song of Solomon 8:6-7 and 2:16** (marriage/delight) and **Genesis 39:9-12** (Joseph fleeing Potiphar's wife) touch romantic/sexual content more directly than anything currently in the library. Handled with restraint they're valuable and age-appropriate (nothing graphic is in the actual verse text), but they should get specific editorial attention in drafting — likely framed toward older teens and adults rather than young children, which is a real difference from most of the existing library's universal accessibility. Worth a decision before Batch 02 whether these stay in scope.
