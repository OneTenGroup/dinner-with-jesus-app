### Dinner 1 — Romans 6:12-14 — "Don't Let Sin Reign"
*New. Theme: what masters us — freedom as an active choice, not just a status.*

**Scripture:** Therefore don't let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts. Neither present your members to sin as instruments of unrighteousness, but present yourselves to God, as alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness to God. For sin will not have dominion over you. For you are not under law, but under grace.

**A Little Context:** Paul writes this to people who are already free in Christ — the instruction isn't about becoming free, it's about what to do with freedom that's already true.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something you do almost automatically, without really deciding to, when you're stressed or bored?

**Go Deeper:** Paul talks about what you "present" yourself to, like handing something over. What do you tend to hand yourself over to without thinking about it?

**Push Further:** Paul says sin won't have dominion over us — not that we'll never be tempted, but that it doesn't get to rule. What's the difference between a struggle you're still in and a master that's still ruling you?

**Prayer:** God, show us what we've been handing ourselves over to without noticing. We want to belong to you instead. Amen.

### Dinner 2 — Matthew 6:24 — "You Cannot Serve Two Masters"
*New. Theme: money as a master, not just a tool.*

**Scripture:** "No one can serve two masters, for either he will hate the one and love the other; or else he will be devoted to one and despise the other. You can't serve both God and Mammon.

**A Little Context:** "Mammon" was a word for money or possessions, personified almost like a rival god. Jesus isn't saying money is evil — he's saying it's powerful enough to actually compete for the position only God should hold.

**For the Table Tonight:** If someone looked only at how you spend your time and money, what would they guess actually matters most to you?

**Go Deeper:** Is there a decision you've made recently where money quietly had more say than you'd like to admit?

**Push Further:** Jesus doesn't say serving money is hard — he says it's impossible to do at the same time as serving God. Why might money specifically, more than most things, demand that kind of total loyalty?

**Prayer:** Lord, we don't want to serve two masters. Show us where money has more of a vote than you do. Amen.

### Dinner 3 — Genesis 2:15 — "Put Him in the Garden to Work"
*New. Theme: work's original dignity, before anything went wrong.*

**Scripture:** Yahweh God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.

**A Little Context:** This happens before anything goes wrong in the story — work isn't a punishment invented later, it's part of the very first, very good world God made.

**For the Table Tonight:** What kind of work — a job, a chore, a project — actually feels satisfying to you when it's done?

**Go Deeper:** Do you usually think of work as something that gets in the way of your real life, or as part of it?

**Push Further:** God gives Adam work before sin ever enters the story. What changes if work is something God built into a perfect world, not just something we have to endure until we get to rest?

**Prayer:** Father, thank you for making work good before it ever got hard. Help us find that goodness in our own work today. Amen.

### Dinner 4 — Luke 22:24-27 — "The One Who Serves"
*New. Theme: what real greatness looks like, at the exact moment ambition flares up.*

**Scripture:** There arose also a contention among them, which of them was considered to be greatest. He said to them, "The kings of the Gentiles lord it over them, and those who have authority over them are called 'benefactors.' But not so with you. But one who is the greater among you, let him become as the younger, and one who is governing, as one who serves. For who is greater, one who sits at the table, or one who serves? Isn't it he who sits at the table? But I am in the midst of you as one who serves.

**A Little Context:** This argument breaks out among the disciples during the Last Supper — one of the most serious moments in the entire Gospels — which makes the timing almost uncomfortable to read.

**For the Table Tonight:** Have you ever caught yourself comparing your own accomplishments to someone else's, right in the middle of something that should've mattered more?

**Go Deeper:** What does "greatness" actually look like among the people you spend the most time with — who gets treated like they matter most?

**Push Further:** Jesus doesn't scold the disciples for wanting to matter — he redefines what mattering actually looks like, using himself as the example. What would change in your own life if you measured greatness by who serves, not who's served?

**Prayer:** Jesus, you served even in the middle of an argument about who was greatest. Teach us that kind of greatness. Amen.

### Dinner 5 — 2 Corinthians 9:6-7 — "A Cheerful Giver"
*New. Theme: generosity as an attitude, not just an amount.*

**Scripture:** Remember this: he who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly. He who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully. Let each man give according as he has determined in his heart; not grudgingly, or under compulsion; for God loves a cheerful giver.

**A Little Context:** Paul is writing about a specific financial collection for a struggling church, but his focus isn't on the total amount raised — it's on the spirit behind each person's decision.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something you've given away — money, time, an object — that felt genuinely good to give, not like an obligation?

**Go Deeper:** When you last gave something away, were you giving because you were supposed to, or because you actually wanted to?

**Push Further:** Paul says God loves a cheerful giver — not just a giver, and not just a generous amount. What does it mean that the attitude behind a gift matters to God as much as the gift itself?

**Prayer:** God, make us cheerful givers, not grudging ones. Show us something to give freely today. Amen.

### Dinner 6 — Titus 2:11-12 — "Grace Teaches Us"
*New. Theme: self-control as something grace produces, not something willpower alone achieves.*

**Scripture:** For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all men, instructing us to the intent that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we would live soberly, righteously, and godly in this present world;

**A Little Context:** Paul doesn't present self-control as a rule imposed from outside — he calls it something grace itself teaches, as a natural outcome of it, not a requirement added on top of it.

**For the Table Tonight:** Has anyone ever taught you something valuable just by how they lived, without ever lecturing you about it?

**Go Deeper:** Do you experience self-control more as pressure from outside yourself, or as something growing from the inside?

**Push Further:** Paul says grace itself teaches self-control — not law, not willpower alone. Which one, in your own experience, actually lasts: forcing yourself, or being taught to want something?

**Prayer:** Father, teach us self-control the way grace is supposed to — not as pressure, but as something we actually want because of what you've done for us. Amen.

### Dinner 7 — Genesis 3:17-19 — "By the Sweat of Your Face"
*New. Theme: work's real difficulty, honestly named.*

**Scripture:** To Adam he said, "Because you have listened to your wife's voice, and have eaten of the tree, of which I commanded you, saying, 'You shall not eat of it,' cursed is the ground for your sake. In toil you will eat of it all the days of your life. Thorns also and thistles will it bring forth to you; and you will eat the herb of the field. By the sweat of your face will you eat bread until you return to the ground, for out of it you were taken. For you are dust, and to dust you shall return."

**A Little Context:** This comes right after Genesis 2:15, where work was simply good. Here, after sin enters the story, that same work becomes genuinely hard — thorns, sweat, toil — without ever being erased as something God gave in the first place.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's the most physically or mentally exhausting kind of work you've ever done?

**Go Deeper:** Do you ever feel like work is fighting against you, not just something you do?

**Push Further:** This curse doesn't take work away — it makes the same good thing from Genesis 2 genuinely harder. What does it mean that work can be both a real gift and a real struggle at the same time, not one or the other?

**Prayer:** God, some of our work is genuinely hard. Meet us in the sweat of it, not just in the moments it feels good. Amen.

### Dinner 8 — 1 Timothy 6:6-10 — "Godliness With Contentment"
*New. Theme: the actual, often-misquoted warning about money's danger.*

**Scripture:** But godliness with contentment is great gain. For we brought nothing into the world, and we certainly can't carry anything out. But having food and clothing, we will be content with that. But those who are determined to be rich fall into a temptation and a snare and many foolish and harmful lusts, such as drown men in ruin and destruction. For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. Some have been led astray from the faith in their greed, and have pierced themselves through with many sorrows.

**A Little Context:** This verse is often misquoted as "money is the root of all evil" — it actually says "the love of money," and specifically warns about the drive to become rich, not about having money itself.

**For the Table Tonight:** Where's the line, for you, between wanting enough and wanting to be rich?

**Go Deeper:** Has chasing more of something — money or anything else — ever cost you more than it gave you?

**Push Further:** Paul says being "determined to be rich" is the trap, not wealth itself. What's the difference between having money and being ruled by the pursuit of it?

**Prayer:** Lord, we don't want to be pierced by chasing more. Teach us contentment as the real gain it actually is. Amen.

### Dinner 9 — Proverbs 30:8-9 — "Give Me Neither Poverty Nor Riches"
*New. Theme: an honest, almost startling prayer for "just enough."*

**Scripture:** Remove far from me falsehood and lies. Give me neither poverty nor riches. Feed me with the food that is needful for me; Lest I be full, deny you, and say, 'Who is Yahweh?' Or lest I be poor, and steal, And so dishonor the name of my God.

**A Little Context:** This is an unusual prayer — most people pray for more, not for "just enough." The writer names a real danger on both ends: too much can breed self-sufficiency, too little can breed desperation.

**For the Table Tonight:** What would "just enough" actually look like for you, specifically?

**Go Deeper:** Have you noticed a connection, in your own life, between how much you have and how much you actually think about God?

**Push Further:** The writer doesn't ask for wealth as a blessing or poverty as humility — he asks for a specific middle amount, because he thinks either extreme could pull him away from God. Do you think he's right that both directions carry real spiritual risk?

**Prayer:** Father, we don't often pray for "just enough." Teach us to actually want it, instead of always wanting more. Amen.

### Dinner 10 — Philippians 3:4-8 — "I Count It All Loss"
*New. Theme: worldly success measured against knowing Christ.*

**Scripture:** though I myself might have confidence even in the flesh. If any other man thinks that he has confidence in the flesh, I yet more: circumcised the eighth day, of the stock of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews; concerning the law, a Pharisee; concerning zeal, persecuting the assembly; concerning the righteousness which is in the law, found blameless. However, what things were gain to me, these have I counted loss for Christ. Yes most assuredly, and I count all things to be loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus, my Lord, for whom I suffered the loss of all things, and count them nothing but refuse, that I may gain Christ

**A Little Context:** Paul is listing his own résumé here — genuinely impressive credentials by every standard of his world — before deliberately setting the whole list aside as worthless compared to what he actually values now.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's an accomplishment you were once really proud of that matters a lot less to you now?

**Go Deeper:** What's on your own version of this list — the credentials or achievements you'd be tempted to lead with if you were trying to impress someone?

**Push Further:** Paul doesn't say his old accomplishments were worthless in themselves — he says he counts them as loss "for Christ," by comparison. What would it take for you to genuinely hold your own achievements that loosely?

**Prayer:** Jesus, help us hold what we've achieved loosely enough to actually see what matters more. Amen.

### Dinner 11 — 2 Peter 2:19 — "Overcome, and So Enslaved"
*New. Theme: what controls you enslaves you, however it's dressed up.*

**Scripture:** promising them liberty, while they themselves are bondservants of corruption; for by whom a man is overcome, by the same is he also brought into bondage.

**A Little Context:** Peter is warning about false teachers who promised freedom while actually being enslaved themselves — the verse's larger point stands on its own: whatever overcomes you becomes your master, no matter what it promised on the way in.

**For the Table Tonight:** Has something that promised to make your life easier ever ended up controlling more of it than you expected?

**Go Deeper:** What's something in your life that started as a choice and now feels more like a habit that chooses for you?

**Push Further:** Peter says being overcome by something is the same as being enslaved to it — not a separate, lesser category. Why might it matter to call it slavery honestly, instead of a softer word like "habit" or "preference"?

**Prayer:** Lord, help us call things what they actually are, instead of softening the truth about what has a hold on us. Amen.

### Dinner 12 — Exodus 20:8-11 — "Remember the Sabbath"
*New. Theme: rest as commanded provision, not an optional extra.*

**Scripture:** "Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy. You shall labor six days, and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to Yahweh your God. You shall not do any work in it, you, nor your son, nor your daughter, your man-servant, nor your maid-servant, nor your cattle, nor your stranger who is within your gates; for in six days Yahweh made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them, and rested the seventh day; therefore Yahweh blessed the Sabbath day, and made it holy.

**A Little Context:** This command covers everyone in the household, including servants and animals — rest isn't treated as a reward for finishing enough work, it's built in as something owed to everyone regardless of how much is left undone.

**For the Table Tonight:** What does an actual day of rest look like for you, if you've ever really had one?

**Go Deeper:** What usually keeps you from resting, even when you technically have the time?

**Push Further:** This command roots rest in God's own example — he rested, not because he was tired, but because rest was part of the pattern from the beginning. What does it mean that rest is commanded, not just permitted?

**Prayer:** God, you built rest into the very rhythm of the world. Help us actually take it instead of just believing in it. Amen.

### Dinner 13 — Luke 14:7-11 — "Take the Lowest Place"
*New. Theme: status-seeking, and what happens when you stop competing for recognition.*

**Scripture:** He spoke a parable to those who were invited, when he noticed how they chose the best seats, and said to them, "When you are invited by anyone to a marriage feast, don't sit in the best seat, since perhaps someone more honorable than you might be invited by him, and he who invited both of you would come and tell you, 'Make room for this person.' Then you would begin, with shame, to take the lowest place. But when you are invited, go and sit in the lowest place, so that when he who invited you comes, he may tell you, 'Friend, move up higher.' Then you will be honored in the presence of all who sit at the table with you. For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted."

**A Little Context:** Jesus tells this after literally watching people jockey for the best seats at a dinner — the story is about an actual social habit he noticed happening in front of him.

**For the Table Tonight:** Have you ever watched people compete for the "best seat," literally or otherwise, at something you were at?

**Go Deeper:** Where in your own life are you angling for recognition instead of just letting your actual contribution speak?

**Push Further:** Jesus isn't just giving etiquette advice — he ends with a much bigger claim about exaltation and humility. Taking the lowest place strategically, hoping to be noticed, and actually not needing the recognition at all can look identical from the outside — how would you tell them apart in yourself?

**Prayer:** Jesus, we don't want to perform humility just to be noticed for it. Teach us the real thing. Amen.

### Dinner 14 — Ecclesiastes 5:10 — "He Who Loves Silver"
*New. Theme: the honest math of "more" never actually satisfying.*

**Scripture:** He who loves silver shall not be satisfied with silver; nor he who loves abundance, with increase: this also is vanity.

**A Little Context:** The Teacher states this as a simple, observed fact about how desire for money actually works — not a moral rule so much as a description of what he's watched happen, over and over.

**For the Table Tonight:** Has getting more of something you wanted ever actually satisfied the wanting, or just moved it somewhere else?

**Go Deeper:** What's something you thought would finally be "enough," and wasn't?

**Push Further:** The Teacher calls this pattern "vanity" — not evil, exactly, but empty, going nowhere. Why might loving money specifically create an appetite that more money can never actually fill?

**Prayer:** God, we've chased "enough" in the wrong places before. Teach us what will actually satisfy. Amen.

### Dinner 15 — Acts 20:35 — "More Blessed to Give"
*New. Theme: a direct saying of Jesus about the actual experience of giving.*

**Scripture:** In all things I gave you an example, that so laboring you ought to help the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, that he himself said, 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.'"

**A Little Context:** This is one of the only sayings of Jesus recorded outside the four Gospels — Paul quotes it from memory, as something already well known among believers by the time he's writing.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something you gave away recently that felt better to give than anything you've received lately?

**Go Deeper:** Do you actually believe giving is more blessed than receiving, or does that feel like something you're supposed to say?

**Push Further:** Jesus doesn't say giving is a duty or a sacrifice — he says it's more blessed, genuinely better, than receiving. What would change about how you give if you actually believed that was true?

**Prayer:** Jesus, we want to believe this, not just repeat it. Show us the blessing in giving, not just the cost. Amen.

### Dinner 16 — 2 Peter 1:5-6 — "Add to Your Faith"

**A Little Context:** Peter lists these qualities as a chain, each one building on the last — self-control isn't presented alone, it's one link connecting knowledge to patience.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's a skill or quality you've actually built over time, one step at a time, rather than getting all at once?

**Go Deeper:** Where in this chain — faith, excellence, knowledge, self-control, patience — do you feel like you're currently working?

**Push Further:** Peter doesn't ask for self-control by itself, disconnected from everything else — it comes from knowledge and leads toward patience. What might change if you stopped trying to will self-control into existence and instead worked on what comes right before it?

**Prayer:** God, build this chain in us, one link at a time. We don't need to master everything today. Amen.

*New. Theme: patterns that quietly become bindings.*

**Scripture:** The evil deeds of the wicked ensnare him. The cords of his sin hold him firmly.

**A Little Context:** The image here is of something that starts as a single action and ends up wound tight enough to actually hold a person in place, like rope.

**For the Table Tonight:** Has a small, repeated choice ever turned into something harder to get out of than you expected?

**Go Deeper:** What's a "cord" in your own life right now — something that started small and has quietly gotten stronger with repetition?

**Push Further:** The proverb doesn't describe one dramatic trap — it describes cords, plural, each one probably thin enough to ignore on its own. Why might the danger of a pattern be easier to miss than the danger of a single bad decision?

**Prayer:** Lord, show us the cords we've stopped noticing because we got used to them. Help us cut what needs to be cut. Amen.

**A Little Context:** God doesn't rest because he's tired — he rests as part of the pattern of creation itself, setting an example long before any command to rest is given to anyone else.

**Go Deeper:** Do you tend to feel guilty when you stop working, even when there's nothing wrong with stopping?

*New. Theme: an uncomfortable story about wealth, poverty, and the gap between them.*

**Scripture:** "Now there was a certain rich man, and he was clothed in purple and fine linen, living in luxury every day. A certain beggar, named Lazarus, was laid at his gate, full of sores, and desiring to be fed with the crumbs that fell from the rich man's table. Yes, even the dogs came and licked his sores. In Hades, he lifted up his eyes, being in torment, and saw Abraham far off, and Lazarus at his bosom. "But Abraham said, 'Son, remember that you, in your lifetime, received your good things, and Lazarus, in like manner, bad things. But now here he is comforted and you are in anguish. **A Little Context:** Jesus doesn't say the rich man did anything actively cruel to Lazarus — the story's discomfort is that he simply never seemed to notice him, a starving man laid at his own gate every day.

**For the Table Tonight:** Is there someone or something right in front of you regularly that's easy not to really notice?

**Go Deeper:** Does never actively harming anyone feel like enough to you, or does this story complicate that?

**Push Further:** The rich man's failure in this story isn't one dramatic act of cruelty — it's an entire lifetime of not noticing a specific person at his own gate. What does that suggest about the kind of failure that's easiest to overlook in yourself?

**Prayer:** Jesus, open our eyes to who's at our own gate — the person right in front of us we've stopped really seeing. Amen.

### Dinner 21 — 1 Kings 17:10-16 — "The Widow's Last Handful"
*New. Theme: generosity out of genuine scarcity, not abundance.*

**Scripture:** So he arose and went to Zarephath; and when he came to the gate of the city, behold, a widow was there gathering sticks: and he called to her, and said, Please get me a little water in a vessel, that I may drink. As she was going to get it, he called to her, and said, Please bring me a morsel of bread in your hand. **For the Table Tonight:** Where does your mind drift to automatically when it's not focused on anything in particular?

**Go Deeper:** What's something "above" — eternal, lasting — that you'd like to think about more than you currently do?

**Push Further:** Paul roots this instruction in something already true ("if you were raised with Christ"), not in effort alone. What would change about your daily focus if you actually believed that starting point was already settled?

**Prayer:** Father, draw our minds toward what actually lasts, not just what's in front of us today. Amen.

### Dinner 47 — Hebrews 13:16 — "Don't Forget to Share"
*New. Theme: generosity as an easy habit to quietly let slip.*

**Scripture:** But don't forget to be doing good and sharing, for with such sacrifices God is well pleased.

**A Little Context:** The writer frames generosity as something easy to simply forget, not refuse — a short reminder tucked among other practical closing instructions.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something generous you meant to do recently and just never got around to?

**Go Deeper:** Is forgetting to be generous different, in your own life, from deciding not to be?

**Push Further:** The writer calls doing good and sharing a "sacrifice" that pleases God — ordinary, easy-to-forget acts, described with the same weight as something offered at an altar. What changes if you thought of small, forgettable generosity as genuine worship?

**Prayer:** God, don't let us forget the good we meant to do. Remind us today, before the moment passes. Amen.
