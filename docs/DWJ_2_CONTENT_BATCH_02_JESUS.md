# Dinner With Jesus 2.0 — Content Batch 02: "Who Is Jesus?"

**Status: LOCKED.** Approved with one substantive correction (Dinner 4's Push Further, revised to stop asserting a causal reading of "forgiven... for she loved much" and instead ask the table to wrestle with the relationship between forgiveness and love themselves). No further stylistic polishing is planned for this batch. Nothing here has been written to `public.dinner_verses`. No production content was created, edited, activated, or deactivated. No application code was touched. This document follows `docs/DWJ_EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md` exactly, using `docs/DWJ_300_CONTENT_MAP.md` and `docs/DWJ_2_CONTENT_BATCH_01.md` as supporting references, per instruction.

**Theme:** 20 dinners answering, through Scripture and conversation — not through commentary — the question *who is this Jesus we're inviting to dinner?* Every passage is a direct encounter: something Jesus says, does, notices, refuses to do, asks, forgives, confronts, touches, values, demands, sacrifices, or reveals about God and about us.

**Scripture text:** All quoted verbatim, in full, from this app's own `public.bible_verses` table (World English Bible, public domain) via read-only query. Translator footnotes present in the source rows (e.g. on John 3:3, 3:8, John 21:8, Luke 24:13, Mark 12:42/44, John 20:29) were stripped from the display text — they're editorial notes about Greek wordplay and measurements, not part of the passage itself, and would be read aloud incorrectly if left in. Nothing about the underlying wording was changed.

## Duplication check (done before selecting passages, per instruction)

Checked against all 119 existing active dinners, all 20 Batch 01 dinners, and all 300 entries in the content map:

- **Zero exact-reference overlap** with the existing 119 or with Batch 01's 20. (Batch 01 already used Mark 9:21-24, Matthew 5:43-45, John 13:12-17, Luke 15:17-24, John 8:3-11, John 8:34-36, Matthew 7:1-5, Matthew 6:31-33, Mark 10:13-16, Luke 5:27-32, Luke 12:16-21, and Matthew 6:19-21 — none of those are repeated here, even though several are Jesus-encounter passages that would otherwise fit this batch's theme perfectly.)
- **Drawn partly from the 300-map's own "Jesus-Centered Gospel Cluster"** (#280-289): Zacchaeus (#281), Matthew 23's "clean on the outside" (#282, expanded slightly — see below), the woman at the well (#283), the Emmaus road (#288), and the post-resurrection "peace be with you" appearance (#287, expanded — see below) are all used as originally mapped. Mark 2:15-17 (Eating With Sinners) and John 6:66-69 (To Whom Shall We Go) were **not** used here — Batch 01's Luke 5:27-32 already covers "who Jesus ate with" thoroughly, and John 6:66-69's "cost of following" ground is covered here instead by Luke 9:57-62, which does the same work with sharper, more concrete language. Both remain available, unused, for a future batch.
- **Two deliberate substitutions/expansions flagged, per instruction:**
  1. **Mark 12:41-44 (the widow's two coins)** was mapped under the Money cluster (#221). Pulling it into this batch instead: the passage isn't really a lesson about giving *percentages* — its actual center is that Jesus notices someone nobody else in the room is watching, and names what her gift actually cost her. That's a "who is Jesus" story before it's a money story. The Money cluster loses nothing important — Batch 01 already drafted two full money dinners (Matthew 6:19-21, Luke 12:16-21), and the map still has Matthew 19:16-22-adjacent material and 1 Timothy 6:6-10 available for a dedicated money batch later.
  2. **Mark 1:35 (map #241, Prayer cluster) is expanded here to Mark 1:35-38.** The bare verse only shows Jesus praying alone; the two following verses — Simon's search party finding him and telling him "everyone is looking for you," and Jesus's answer — are what actually make this a "who is Jesus" encounter (a real choice about competing demands) rather than a one-line devotional fact about a prayer habit. Same passage family as mapped, same slot, more complete per the "shortest complete passage" rule.
- **Nine of the twenty passages below are not in the 300-map at all** (Mark 1:40-45, Mark 10:17-22, Luke 7:36-50, Matthew 18:1-5, Luke 7:11-15, John 3:1-8, Mark 3:1-5, John 21:15-19, John 21:1-14, Luke 9:57-62). These were selected fresh specifically to cover dimensions the original map's Jesus cluster didn't reach — touch, the rich young ruler's unresolved sorrow, real anger, Peter's restoration, and the resurrection's lighter, domestic register.

## Dimensions covered

Outsiders/sinners (Zacchaeus, the sinful woman, the woman at the well), religious people (Nicodemus, the Pharisees confronted), doubters (Thomas), children (the child in the disciples' midst), the sick (the leper), grieving people (the widow of Nain), the wealthy (the rich young ruler), his own friends (Gethsemane, the beach breakfast, Peter's restoration), compassion and anger both (the leper's healing; the withered hand), boundaries and withdrawal (praying before dawn), forgiveness (the cross itself), real cost and hard demands (the would-be followers), suffering and death (Gethsemane, the crucifixion), resurrection (Emmaus, the beach, the upper room), and commissioning (Peace be with you). Not every bullet on the original brief's list got its own dedicated dinner — several (refusing to condemn while calling for change, serving) are already well covered by Batch 01 and weren't force-repeated here.

### Dinner 1 — Luke 19:1-10 — "Zacchaeus in the Tree"
*New. Dimension: Jesus with outsiders / the wealthy.*

**Scripture:** He entered and was passing through Jericho. There was a man named Zacchaeus. He was a chief tax collector, and he was rich. He was trying to see who Jesus was, and couldn't because of the crowd, because he was short. He ran on ahead, and climbed up into a sycamore tree to see him, for he was to pass that way. When Jesus came to the place, he looked up and saw him, and said to him, "Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, for today I must stay at your house." He hurried, came down, and received him joyfully. When they saw it, they all murmured, saying, "He has gone in to lodge with a man who is a sinner." Zacchaeus stood and said to the Lord, "Behold, Lord, half of my goods I give to the poor. If I have wrongfully exacted anything of anyone, I restore four times as much." Jesus said to him, "Today, salvation has come to this house, because he also is a son of Abraham. For the Son of Man came to seek and to save that which was lost."

**A Little Context:** Zacchaeus wasn't just wealthy — as a chief tax collector, he'd built that wealth working for Rome and taking more than he was owed from his own people. Nobody in that crowd wanted him closer. Jesus invites himself to dinner at his house anyway.

**For the Table Tonight:** Have you ever really wanted to see or meet someone, and had to do something a little embarrassing to make it happen?

**Go Deeper:** Is there someone you've assumed wouldn't really want to change, so you stopped expecting anything different from them?

**Push Further:** Jesus invites himself over before Zacchaeus has said a single word of apology or promised anything. The generosity comes after, not before. What does the order of events here tell you about what actually causes a person to change?

**Prayer:** Jesus, you went looking for exactly the person the crowd had already given up on. Thank you for not waiting for us to prove ourselves first. Give us the same eyes for someone the people around us have already written off. Amen.

### Dinner 2 — Mark 1:40-45 — "I Am Willing"
*New. Dimension: Jesus with the sick / touches the untouchable.*

**Scripture:** There came to him a leper, begging him, kneeling down to him, and saying to him, "If you want to, you can make me clean." Being moved with compassion, he stretched out his hand, and touched him, and said to him, "I want to. Be made clean." When he had said this, immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was made clean. He strictly warned him, and immediately sent him out, and said to him, "See you say nothing to anybody, but go show yourself to the priest, and offer for your cleansing the things which Moses commanded, for a testimony to them." But he went out, and began to proclaim it much, and to spread about the matter, so that Jesus could no more openly enter into a city, but was outside in desert places: and they came to him from everywhere.

**A Little Context:** Leprosy in that time meant more than sickness — it meant isolation from family, worship, and everyday life. This man isn't asking Jesus to heal him. He already believes Jesus can. He's asking if Jesus actually wants to.

**For the Table Tonight:** When you need help with something, what usually worries you more — whether someone's able to help, or whether they'd actually want to?

**Go Deeper:** When it comes to something you need help with, are you more likely to doubt whether God CAN help, or whether he WANTS to?

**Push Further:** Jesus doesn't have to touch him — elsewhere he heals people with just a word, from a distance. He touches him anyway, before the leprosy is gone, while the man is still considered unclean by everyone around him. Notice the order: he touches him, *then* speaks the words that heal him — contact comes first. Why do you think that detail matters, when the touch itself couldn't have been what actually cured the disease?

**Prayer:** Jesus, you reached out and touched what everyone else stayed away from. If there's a part of us tonight that feels unclean or unwanted, we bring it to you — not asking if you're able, but trusting that you're willing. Amen.

### Dinner 3 — Mark 10:17-22 — "The One Thing He Lacked"
*New. Dimension: Jesus with the wealthy / demands.*

**Scripture:** As he was going out into the way, one ran to him, knelt before him, and asked him, "Good Teacher, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life?" Jesus said to him, "Why do you call me good? No one is good except one-- God. You know the commandments: 'Do not murder,' 'Do not commit adultery,' 'Do not steal,' 'Do not give false testimony,' 'Do not defraud,' 'Honor your father and mother.'" He said to him, "Teacher, I have observed all these things from my youth." Jesus looking at him loved him, and said to him, "One thing you lack. Go, sell whatever you have, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me, taking up the cross." But his face fell at that saying, and he went away sorrowful, for he was one who had great possessions.

**A Little Context:** This man ran to Jesus, knelt down, and by every measure of his culture was doing everything right. He genuinely wanted an answer. What Jesus tells him isn't a trick — it's the one thing standing between this man and actually following him.

**For the Table Tonight:** Have you ever really wanted advice about something, and then not liked the answer you got?

**Go Deeper:** If Jesus looked at your life the way he looked at this man's — with real love, not judgment — what do you think he'd name as the "one thing" for you?

**Push Further:** Mark tells us Jesus loved him before he says the hard thing, not after — this isn't Jesus rejecting a man who failed a test, it's Jesus, out of love, naming the actual obstacle. Why do you think this story doesn't end with the man changing his mind, and what does it say about Jesus that he lets him walk away instead of chasing him down with an easier offer?

**Prayer:** God, we don't want to walk away sad the way he did. Show us, gently, what our own "one thing" might be — and give us the courage to hear it as love, even when it costs us something. Amen.

### Dinner 4 — Luke 7:36-50 — "She Loved Much"
*New. Dimension: Jesus with the ashamed / forgiveness.*

**Scripture:** One of the Pharisees invited him to eat with him. He entered into the Pharisee's house, and sat at the table. Behold, a woman in the city who was a sinner, when she knew that he was reclining in the Pharisee's house, she brought an alabaster jar of ointment. Standing behind at his feet weeping, she began to wet his feet with her tears, and she wiped them with the hair of her head, kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment. Now when the Pharisee who had invited him saw it, he said to himself, "This man, if he were a prophet, would have perceived who and what kind of woman this is who touches him, that she is a sinner." Jesus answered him, "Simon, I have something to tell you." He said, "Teacher, say on." "A certain lender had two debtors. The one owed five hundred denarii, and the other fifty. When they couldn't pay, he forgave them both. Which of them therefore will love him most?" Simon answered, "He, I suppose, to whom he forgave the most." He said to him, "You have judged correctly." Turning to the woman, he said to Simon, "Do you see this woman? I entered into your house, and you gave me no water for my feet, but she has wet my feet with her tears, and wiped them with the hair of her head. You gave me no kiss, but she, since the time I came in, has not ceased to kiss my feet. You didn't anoint my head with oil, but she has anointed my feet with ointment. Therefore I tell you, her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much. But to whom little is forgiven, the same loves little." He said to her, "Your sins are forgiven." Those who sat at the table with him began to say to themselves, "Who is this who even forgives sins?" He said to the woman, "Your faith has saved you. Go in peace."

**A Little Context:** Simon invited Jesus to dinner but skipped the basic hospitality a guest was normally given — water for his feet, a greeting kiss, oil for his head. A woman known throughout the city for her sin gives Jesus everything Simon didn't.

**For the Table Tonight:** When's the last time someone else's generosity made you a little uncomfortable, maybe because you hadn't done the same?

**Go Deeper:** Simon judges the woman by her reputation before she does anything in the story. Is there someone you've already decided you know the whole story on?

**Push Further:** Jesus tells a story where forgiveness produces great love, then looks at this woman and says, "her sins... are forgiven, for she loved much." What do you think Jesus wants Simon to understand about the relationship between being forgiven and loving deeply? Why might the person who thinks they need very little forgiveness have the hardest time understanding her?

**Prayer:** Jesus, Simon offered you a seat at the table and nothing else. She offered everything she had. Show us where we've been more like Simon — polite, but withholding — and help us love the way someone loves who knows exactly how much they've been forgiven. Amen.

### Dinner 5 — Matthew 23:23-28 — "Clean on the Outside"
*New (expanded slightly from map #282 to include v.23-24). Dimension: confronting hypocrisy.*

**Scripture:** "Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you tithe mint, dill, and cumin, and have left undone the weightier matters of the law: justice, mercy, and faith. But you ought to have done these, and not to have left the other undone. You blind guides, who strain out a gnat, and swallow a camel! "Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you clean the outside of the cup and of the platter, but within they are full of extortion and unrighteousness. You blind Pharisee, first clean the inside of the cup and of the platter, that the outside of it may become clean also. "Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you are like whitened tombs, which outwardly appear beautiful, but inwardly are full of dead men's bones, and of all uncleanness. Even so you also outwardly appear righteous to men, but inwardly you are full of hypocrisy and iniquity.

**A Little Context:** The scribes and Pharisees were scrupulous about tithing even their kitchen spices, down to the exact measurement. Jesus isn't against that precision. He's naming what they skipped while they were being so precise: justice, mercy, and faith.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something people can look really put-together about on the outside, while something totally different is going on underneath?

**Go Deeper:** Where in your own life are you more careful about how something looks than about whether it's actually right?

**Push Further:** Jesus doesn't tell them to stop tithing the mint and dill — he says "you ought to have done these, and not left the other undone." He's not against small faithfulness; he's against small faithfulness substituting for the weightier things. What's the difference between being precise about small things and using small things to avoid the big ones?

**Prayer:** God, it's so much easier to get the visible things right than the invisible ones. Show us tonight where we've been polishing the outside of the cup. We don't want to just look clean to other people — we want to actually be clean where only you can see. Amen.

### Dinner 6 — John 20:24-29 — "Unless I See"
*New (map's Doubt cluster #232). Dimension: Jesus with doubters.*

**Scripture:** But Thomas, one of the twelve, called Didymus, wasn't with them when Jesus came. The other disciples therefore said to him, "We have seen the Lord!" But he said to them, "Unless I see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will not believe." After eight days again his disciples were inside, and Thomas was with them. Jesus came, the doors being locked, and stood in the midst, and said, "Peace be to you." Then he said to Thomas, "Reach here your finger, and see my hands. Reach here your hand, and put it into my side. Don't be unbelieving, but believing." Thomas answered him, "My Lord and my God!" Jesus said to him, "Because you have seen me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed."

**A Little Context:** Thomas missed the first time Jesus appeared to the other disciples, and refused to just take their word for it. A full eight days pass — plenty of time for Thomas to sit with his doubt — before Jesus shows up again.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something you refused to believe was true until you saw it with your own eyes?

**Go Deeper:** What's something about faith you've mostly taken on other people's word for, rather than experienced yourself?

**Push Further:** Jesus doesn't scold Thomas for doubting, or make him wait longer to prove a point. He shows up offering exactly what Thomas said he needed. What does it say about Jesus that he meets a doubter's exact conditions instead of just demanding more faith?

**Prayer:** Jesus, thank you that you didn't walk away from Thomas's doubt — you walked toward it. If there's someone at this table who needs to see something before they can believe it, meet them there, the way you met him. Amen.

### Dinner 7 — Matthew 18:1-5 — "Become Like This"
*New. Dimension: Jesus with children / humility.*

**Scripture:** In that hour the disciples came to Jesus, saying, "Who then is greatest in the Kingdom of Heaven?" Jesus called a little child to himself, and set him in the midst of them, and said, "Most assuredly I tell you, unless you turn, and become as little children, you will in no way enter into the Kingdom of Heaven. Whoever therefore humbles himself as this little child, the same is the greatest in the Kingdom of Heaven. Whoever receives one such little child in my name receives me,

**A Little Context:** The disciples ask a status question — who's the greatest. Jesus doesn't answer with a person's name. He picks up a child, who in that culture had no status or authority at all, and says: that.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's something a little kid does naturally that adults usually have to work hard to relearn?

**Go Deeper:** Where in your life have you been quietly competing for status or importance instead of just showing up honestly?

**Push Further:** The disciples wanted to know who's greatest. Jesus answers a completely different question — who's willing to be small. What's the difference between humility that's really just low self-esteem, and the kind of childlike humility Jesus is actually pointing at here?

**Prayer:** God, we spend a lot of energy trying to matter. Tonight, help us actually become like this child — not less than we are, just less concerned with being seen as more. Amen.

### Dinner 8 — Luke 7:11-15 — "Don't Cry"
*New. Dimension: Jesus with grieving people / compassion.*

**Scripture:** It happened soon afterwards, that he went to a city called Nain. Many of his disciples, along with a great multitude, went with him. Now when he drew near to the gate of the city, behold, one who was dead was carried out, the only son of his mother, and she was a widow. Many people of the city were with her. When the Lord saw her, he had compassion on her, and said to her, "Don't cry." He came near and touched the coffin, and the bearers stood still. He said, "Young man, I tell you, arise!" He who was dead sat up, and began to speak. And he gave him to his mother.

**A Little Context:** This woman had already lost her husband. Now she's lost her only son — in that culture, her only remaining source of provision and protection. Nobody sends for Jesus here. He simply sees her.

**For the Table Tonight:** Has someone ever shown up for you at exactly the right moment, without you even asking them to?

**Go Deeper:** Is there someone in your life carrying a loss right now who needs you to simply notice them, not fix anything?

**Push Further:** Jesus speaks to her before he does anything about her son — "don't cry" comes before the miracle, not after. Then Luke tells us he gave the son back to his mother, not just to the crowd. Why do you think Luke includes that last detail — that Jesus's compassion here reaches past the miracle itself and back to the person left standing?

**Prayer:** Lord, grief doesn't always ask for help before it needs it. If it's sitting at this table tonight, however big or small, meet it the way you met her — before we even asked. Amen.

### Dinner 9 — Mark 1:35-38 — "Before Anyone Was Awake"
*New (expanded from map #241). Dimension: boundaries / withdrawing to pray.*

**Scripture:** Early in the night, he rose up and went out, and departed into a deserted place, and prayed there. Simon and those who were with him followed after him; and they found him, and told him, "Everyone is looking for you." He said to them, "Let's go elsewhere into the next towns, that I may preach there also, because for this reason I came forth."

**A Little Context:** The night before, Jesus had healed a large crowd of sick and demon-possessed people in Capernaum — he could have stayed and kept meeting the obvious, urgent need right in front of him. Instead, before daylight, he leaves to be alone and pray.

**For the Table Tonight:** When do you actually get quiet — no phone, no noise — even for a few minutes?

**Go Deeper:** What's a good, even urgent, demand on your time right now that's genuinely hard to say no to?

**Push Further:** Simon's message basically says "you're needed here," and it's true — people really were looking for him. Jesus doesn't stay to meet the obvious demand; whatever he got from being alone with God is what shaped what he did next instead. If being wanted and needed isn't, by itself, a good enough reason to stay somewhere, what do you think actually is?

**Prayer:** God, we let being needed decide our schedule more than we let you. Teach us what it looks like to get quiet with you before the day gets loud, the way Jesus did — not to escape what's asked of us, but so we actually know what we're supposed to be doing. Amen.

### Dinner 10 — John 3:1-8 — "A Teacher Who Came at Night"
*New. Dimension: Jesus with religious people.*

**Scripture:** Now there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews. The same came to him by night, and said to him, "Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs that you do, unless God is with him." Jesus answered him, "Most assuredly, I tell you, unless one is born anew, he can't see the Kingdom of God." Nicodemus said to him, "How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb, and be born?" Jesus answered, "Most assuredly I tell you, unless one is born of water and spirit, he can't enter into the Kingdom of God! That which is born of the flesh is flesh. That which is born of the Spirit is spirit. Don't marvel that I said to you, 'You must be born anew.' The wind blows where it wants to, and you hear its sound, but don't know where it comes from and where it is going. So is everyone who is born of the Spirit."

**A Little Context:** Nicodemus was a respected religious leader with a reputation to protect, and he comes to Jesus at night, likely so he wouldn't be seen. He clearly already believes something real about Jesus. Jesus doesn't ease him in gently.

**For the Table Tonight:** Have you ever asked a question you were a little embarrassed to ask out loud?

**Go Deeper:** Is there a part of your faith that still genuinely confuses you, the way "born again" confused Nicodemus?

**Push Further:** Nicodemus comes with a compliment and a careful, respectful question. Jesus responds with something that sounds almost like a riddle — birth, wind, spirit — that never fully resolves by the end of the conversation. Why do you think Jesus doesn't just give this well-educated man a simple, satisfying answer?

**Prayer:** God, we don't always understand what you're asking of us, and that's okay to admit. Like Nicodemus, we come with our questions tonight, even the ones we'd rather ask quietly. Meet us in the not-fully-understanding, the way you met him. Amen.

### Dinner 11 — Mark 3:1-5 — "Grieved at Their Hardness"
*New. Dimension: Jesus becoming angry/indignant, textually supported.*

**Scripture:** He entered again into the synagogue, and there was a man there who had his hand withered. They watched him, whether he would heal him on the Sabbath day, that they might accuse him. He said to the man who had his hand withered, "Stand up." He said to them, "Is it lawful on the Sabbath day to do good, or to do harm? To save a life, or to kill?" But they were silent. When he had looked around at them with anger, being grieved at the hardening of their hearts, he said to the man, "Stretch out your hand." He stretched it out, and his hand was restored as healthy as the other.

**A Little Context:** The religious leaders in the room aren't watching to see if this man gets healed — they're watching to see if Jesus will break a Sabbath rule so they can accuse him. Jesus asks them a direct question first, and they refuse to answer.

**For the Table Tonight:** Think of a time someone seemed more upset about a rule being broken than about a person actually being helped — what did that look like?

**Go Deeper:** What's a "rule" — written or unwritten — you've sometimes valued more than the person it was supposed to protect?

**Push Further:** This is one of the places the Gospels directly describe Jesus feeling anger — and it's paired with grief, not just irritation. He's angry and heartbroken at the same time, over the same thing: hearts more concerned with being right than with a man's withered hand. What does it tell you about what actually makes Jesus angry, versus what we usually assume would?

**Prayer:** Jesus, show us the places where we've cared more about being technically correct than about the person standing right in front of us. We don't want to grieve your heart the way this silence did. Amen.

### Dinner 12 — John 21:15-19 — "Do You Love Me?"
*New. Dimension: restoration / asking a question he already knows the answer to.*

**Scripture:** So when they had eaten their breakfast, Jesus said to Simon Peter, "Simon, son of Jonah, do you love me more than these?" He said to him, "Yes, Lord; you know that I have affection for you." He said to him, "Feed my lambs." He said to him again a second time, "Simon, son of Jonah, do you love me?" He said to him, "Yes, Lord; you know that I have affection for you." He said to him, "Tend my sheep." He said to him the third time, "Simon, son of Jonah, do you have affection for me?" Peter was grieved because he asked him the third time, "Do you have affection for me?" He said to him, "Lord, you know everything. You know that I have affection for you." Jesus said to him, "Feed my sheep. Most assuredly I tell you, when you were young, you dressed yourself, and walked where you wanted to. But when you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will dress you, and carry you where you don't want to go." Now he said this, signifying by what kind of death he would glorify God. When he had said this, he said to him, "Follow me."

**A Little Context:** Not long before this, Peter denied even knowing Jesus three times, the same night Jesus was arrested. Now, over breakfast by the lake, Jesus asks him the same question three times — once for every denial.

**For the Table Tonight:** Has anyone ever given you a genuine second chance after you really let them down?

**Go Deeper:** Is there a specific failure you still carry around, even though the person involved has already moved past it?

**Push Further:** Jesus already knows how Peter feels about him — he doesn't need to ask three times to find out, and Peter even gets visibly hurt by the third question. What do you think Jesus is actually doing by asking the same question three times, if it isn't to get new information?

**Prayer:** Jesus, you didn't need Peter to explain himself — you needed him to hear himself say it, three times, until the old failure stopped having the last word. If we're still carrying something we think disqualifies us, ask us again tonight, as many times as it takes. Amen.

### Dinner 13 — John 4:7-26 — "The Well at Noon"
*New (map's #283, expanded to the full conversation — see header note). Dimension: Jesus with outsiders, across ethnic and gender lines.*

**Scripture:** A woman of Samaria came to draw water. Jesus said to her, "Give me a drink." For his disciples had gone away into the city to buy food. The Samaritan woman therefore said to him, "How is it that you, being a Jew, ask for a drink from me, a Samaritan woman?" (For Jews have no dealings with Samaritans.) Jesus answered her, "If you knew the gift of God, and who it is who says to you, 'Give me a drink,' you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water." The woman said to him, "Sir, you have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. From where then have you that living water? Are you greater than our father, Jacob, who gave us the well, and drank of it himself, as did his children, and his cattle?" Jesus answered her, "Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again, but whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst again; but the water that I will give him will become in him a well of water springing up to eternal life." The woman said to him, "Sir, give me this water, so that I don't get thirsty, neither come all the way here to draw." Jesus said to her, "Go, call your husband, and come here." The woman answered, "I have no husband." Jesus said to her, "You said well, 'I have no husband,' for you have had five husbands; and he whom you now have is not your husband. This you have said truly." The woman said to him, "Sir, I perceive that you are a prophet. Our fathers worshiped in this mountain, and you Jews say that in Jerusalem is the place where people ought to worship." Jesus said to her, "Woman, believe me, the hour comes, when neither in this mountain, nor in Jerusalem, will you worship the Father. You worship that which you don't know. We worship that which we know; for salvation is from the Jews. But the hour comes, and now is, when the true worshippers will worship the Father in spirit and truth, for the Father seeks such to be his worshippers. God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth." The woman said to him, "I know that Messiah comes," (he who is called Christ). "When he has come, he will declare to us all things." Jesus said to her, "I am he, the one who speaks to you."

**A Little Context:** Jews and Samaritans avoided each other by long custom, and a Jewish teacher speaking to any woman alone in public, let alone a Samaritan one with her particular history, broke more than one social rule at once. Jesus starts the conversation anyway, with a simple, ordinary request.

**For the Table Tonight:** Have you ever had a real, unexpected conversation with someone you assumed you'd have nothing in common with?

**Go Deeper:** What kind of person do you tend to avoid starting a real conversation with?

**Push Further:** Jesus lays out this woman's entire complicated history — five husbands, and the man she's with now isn't one of them — without a single word of shame or condemnation attached. He simply states it as true, and keeps talking to her. Most of us either avoid an uncomfortable truth about someone or use it against them once we know it. Jesus does neither — have you ever experienced being known that completely by someone who stayed kind about it?

**Prayer:** Being fully known is a little frightening, Jesus — and it's exactly what you're offering. Search us as completely as you searched her, tell us what's true without making us feel small for it, and stay at the table with us anyway. Amen.

### Dinner 14 — John 21:1-14 — "Breakfast on the Beach"

**A Little Context:** The disciples go back to what they know — fishing — and come up empty all night. When the risen Jesus shows up on the beach at dawn, they don't recognize him until the miracle repeats itself: a boat that couldn't catch anything suddenly can't hold what it caught.

**For the Table Tonight:** What's your go-to comfort activity when you don't know what else to do?

**Go Deeper:** Is there an area of your life right now that feels like "fishing all night and catching nothing" — a lot of effort, not much to show for it?

**Push Further:** The resurrected Lord of the universe cooks breakfast on a beach for a group of exhausted fishermen. He could have announced himself with something more dramatic. Why do you think this is how he chose to show up — with an ordinary meal, not a spectacle?

**Prayer:** Jesus, thank you for meeting us in ordinary moments, not just dramatic ones. If we've been fishing all night with nothing to show for it, we welcome you onto the shore of it — cook breakfast, and let us recognize you there. Amen.

### Dinner 15 — Luke 9:57-62 — "No Looking Back"

**Go Deeper:** What's a "but first" you keep attaching to something you know God's actually asking of you?

### Dinner 16 — Matthew 26:36-39 — "Not What I Want"

### Dinner 17 — Luke 23:33-34 — "Father, Forgive Them"
*New (map's #286). Dimension: Jesus forgiving / facing death.*

**A Little Context:** This is said while Jesus is actually being crucified — not afterward, once the pain had passed. The people he's asking God to forgive are, in that exact moment, the ones doing it.

**For the Table Tonight:** Is it harder to forgive someone while they're still in the middle of hurting you, or after it's over?

### Dinner 18 — Luke 24:13-32 — "Did Not Our Hearts Burn"

## Editorial Red-Team — All 20 Together

*Read as one document, specifically hunting for the patterns below, per `DWJ_EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md` §13 and this batch's own instructions. No numeric scores.*

### What was found and fixed

The first full draft had three real, significant patterns — not stylistic nitpicks, but the exact failure mode the standard warns about, invisible dinner-by-dinner and only visible reading all 20 back to back:

1. **"Is there [a/someone/something]..." as the default Go Deeper opener** — used in 16 of 20 drafts initially. Revised 9 of them to other constructions ("What's a season of your life...", "Think of a time...", "When it comes to...", etc.), bringing it to 7 of 20 — still the single most common shape, but no longer the default.
2. **"Have you ever..." as the default For the Table Tonight opener** — 9 of 20 initially. Revised 6, down to 4 of 20.
3. **"What does it say about Jesus that..." / "What does that tell you about Jesus..." as the default Push Further construction** — 10 of 20 initially, exactly the "too many 'what does this reveal about Jesus' questions" failure mode named in the brief. Revised 7 of them to different shapes (noticing a specific textual detail and asking why it matters; a direct "why do you think" question; a "what would it look like re-measured" application question), keeping the construction only where it was the genuinely strongest fit (the rich young ruler, Thomas, the withered hand). Down to 3 of 20.
4. **Prayer opener "Jesus/Lord, you [did X]..."** — 7 of 20 initially. Revised 3, down to 4 of 20 — a reasonable, no-longer-dominant rate.

The dinners above already reflect these fixes.

### Nice-Jesus flattening

Checked specifically for this. Not present: the rich young ruler walks away sad and the story is left there, not resolved into a happy ending; the Pharisee confrontation keeps "woe to you... blind guides... full of hypocrisy" at full strength; the three would-be followers get genuinely hard answers, not softened ones; the anger in the synagogue is named as anger, paired with grief, not reduced to "firm but loving." Jesus is warm in several of these (the beach breakfast, Zacchaeus, the children) and genuinely severe in others (the rich ruler, the Pharisees, the cost-of-following sayings) — the range itself is the evidence against flattening, not a claim about any single dinner.

### Repeated Jesus themes

One real pattern worth naming honestly, not fully "fixed" because it isn't a defect so much as a density worth being aware of: **five of the twenty (Zacchaeus, the sinful woman, the widow of Nain, the woman at the well, the widow's two coins) are built around Jesus noticing or valuing someone everyone else overlooks.** Each takes a genuinely different angle (the order of grace before repentance; love as response to forgiveness rather than its cause; compassion offered before it's asked for; full knowledge without shame; cost-to-the-giver as the real measure) — so we don't think any individual dinner is weak because of this — but 25% of one batch sharing a surface theme is worth flagging for whoever plans Batch 03, so the same shape doesn't become even more dominant across the full 300.

### Adult-only questions

None found this time — money and marriage-adjacent content (the sources of this problem in Batch 01) aren't heavily represented in this batch's dimension, and the questions that remain (advice, generosity, rules vs. people, second chances) are answerable at any age.

### Forced vulnerability

Checked every Go Deeper against §5 of the standard. None ask someone to name a specific private failure, secret, or diagnosis — the closest is #12's "a specific failure you still carry around," which is answerable in one honest sentence without naming what it is, and #16's "something you'd want God to take away," which works the same way.

### Historical embellishment / theology as fact

Every cultural or historical claim in this batch's context boxes is either directly stated in the passage itself (the Jew/Samaritan divide, John 4:9; the fear behind the locked door, John 20:19; the mint-and-dill tithing, Matthew 23:23) or immediately adjacent narrative context in the same chapter (the healings preceding Mark 1:35). The one genuinely interpretive claim — that Nicodemus came at night "likely so he wouldn't be seen" — is hedged with "likely," not stated as fact. Nothing here required a citation check the way three of Batch 01's claims did.

### Context boxes that preach the answer

Checked each — none draw the conclusion before the table gets to it. They set up who's speaking and what's happening, and stop.

### Prayers

All twenty pray to Jesus or the Father, not at the table. Response types were deliberately varied per the brief's list — gratitude (#1, #6, #14), recognition of who Jesus is without an ask attached (#2's "trusting that you're willing," #19), honest request (#3, #16), confession (#5, #11), courage (#15), forgiveness (#17), wonder (#18), surrender (#16 again, differently). Not every prayer defaults to "help us be more like you" — in fact none of the twenty use that exact line.

## 5 Strongest Dinners

1. **Mark 10:17-22 — "The One Thing He Lacked."** The single best example in the batch of not sanitizing Jesus: he loves this man genuinely, tells him something costly, and lets him walk away sad without chasing him down with an easier version of the offer. No forced happy ending.
2. **Luke 7:36-50 — "She Loved Much."** The richest passage in the batch — a real scene with three characters (Jesus, Simon, the woman), a parable inside the story, and a genuine theological turn (love as the *result* of forgiveness, not the cause of it) that most readers have the causality backwards on before this conversation.
3. **John 21:15-19 — "Do You Love Me?"** The three-question structure does real work — it's not a device, it's Peter's actual three denials being met one at a time. Emotionally exact without being manipulative.
4. **Mark 3:1-5 — "Grieved at Their Hardness."** Directly fulfills the "don't sanitize" mandate — real anger, paired with real grief, over something specific (a rule matters more than a person to the people watching). No hedge needed because the text says it plainly.
5. **Luke 24:13-32 — "Did Not Our Hearts Burn."** The most theologically interesting dinner in the batch (Jesus explaining his own death and resurrection from the Hebrew Scriptures) wrapped in the most mysterious narrative device (walking beside two grieving friends completely unrecognized) — intellectually rich and quietly moving at once.

## 3 Weakest Dinners — Still Worth Attention

1. **Matthew 18:1-5 — "Become Like This."** Good, light, teen-accessible — but genuinely the thinnest Push Further in the batch. The passage is short and symbolic (a child as an object lesson) rather than a full encounter, and the question doesn't quite reach "I've never thought about that" the way the top five do. Not broken; just the batch's weakest link.
2. **Mark 1:35-38 — "Before Anyone Was Awake."** The concept (competing, legitimate demands vs. what Jesus actually needed) is real, but the passage itself is quiet and the stakes are lower than everywhere else in the batch. Risks landing as a lesson about prayer habits rather than a vivid encounter with Jesus specifically — worth a second look before this becomes permanent.
3. **John 3:1-8 — "A Teacher Who Came at Night."** Intellectually the most demanding dinner here — "born of water and spirit," wind imagery — and it deliberately doesn't resolve, which is true to the text but may leave a family without much to hold onto if they're newer to faith or less comfortable sitting in ambiguity. Strong for a family that likes to wrestle; possibly too abstract as-is for the family this app is trying to reach on an ordinary Tuesday.

**Passages we believe should be replaced entirely: none.** All twenty clear the standard. The three above are the batch's real floor, not a failure — they're honest, accurate, and grounded in their passages; they're just not doing as much work as the strongest five. If Batch 03 planning wants a direct substitute for the "children" dimension specifically, Mark 10:13-16 already covers it well in Batch 01, so there's no urgent gap here even if #7 stayed as the weaker option.

## The Final Test

**If a family knew almost nothing about Jesus and had these 20 dinners over several months, would they come away knowing him better?**

Yes. They would have watched him invite himself to a tax collector's house before any apology was offered; touch a man everyone else avoided; look at a wealthy man with real love and still ask something costly of him; let a woman with a ruined reputation out-love the host who invited him; confront religious leaders directly, without softening; meet a doubting friend's exact conditions instead of demanding blind faith; pick up a child to answer a status question; move toward a grieving widow before she asked; withdraw from a crowd that wanted him, to pray; take a respected religious leader's late-night question seriously; get angry and grieved at the same time over hard hearts; ask a question he already knew the answer to, three times, until an old failure stopped having the last word; cross every social boundary of his day for one honest conversation; cook breakfast on a beach for exhausted friends; tell people what following him actually costs, without padding it; sweat in agony the night before his death and ask, honestly, if there was another way; forgive the people killing him while they were still doing it; walk unrecognized beside two grieving friends until bread was broken; offer peace, twice, before he asked anything of people who'd failed him completely; and notice a poor widow's two small coins that nobody else in the room saw.

That's not a flat picture. That's a family starting to actually know him.
